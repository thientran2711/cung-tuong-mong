ROOM: Vĩnh Hòa cung chính điện bedroom, the private bedchamber of a Tần (an imperial consort of the fourth rank, below a Phi) who heads this palace and lives here alone, in ancient China, a real room in daily use, seen with THREE walls: the back wall straight across the middle of the picture and the left and right side walls running towards the viewer in clear perspective, meeting it at square corners, like the wall layout of image 3: pillars, lattice windows in the LEFT side wall onto a sunny garden; against the back wall in its RIGHT half, square to it and seen straight on, ONE canopy bed, the large clear main subject: a wide canopy bed of carved warm reddish-brown jichimu wood with soft pink gauze curtains tied back and soft pink silk bedding embroidered with camellias; in the LEFT half of the back wall the gourd-shaped doorway in a lattice partition of overlapping lozenges onto an inner room; along the left window wall, square to it, a dressing table with a bronze mirror and a small boxwood jewellery box and a round stool with a soft pink cushion; in the right side wall a tall clothes cabinet, a clothes rack with a soft pink robe beside the bed, pots of camellias on slim stands, hanging paper lanterns with tassels. Vĩnh Hòa cung signature style, the same in every room of this palace: camellias (hoa trà) are its flower, painted on the screens, embroidered on the silks and in the pots; motifs of pairs of orioles on bamboo stems carved in the beams and furniture; warm reddish-brown jichimu wood with small carved boxwood inlays; southern official's-hat armchairs with a gently curved top rail that does not stick out; a gourd-shaped doorway in a lattice partition of overlapping lozenges.
