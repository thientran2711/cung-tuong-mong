## 1. History (Qing main, Ming secondary)
Caveat: written from standard references (《大清會典》, 《清史稿·后妃传》, 《明史·后妃传》, Qing 《實錄》 entries). I did not re-verify by live web search; dates marked ~ are approximate.

**1.1 Qing — structure.** Eight grades: Hoàng hậu · Hoàng quý phi · Quý phi · Phi · Tần · Quý nhân · Thường tại · Đáp ứng (fixed by Qianlong's era; Kangxi/Yongzheng had fewer, looser titles). Seats were capped by custom (1 Hoàng hậu, 1 Hoàng quý phi, 2 Quý phi, 4 Phi, 6 Tần; lower grades uncapped). The harem was run by Nội vụ phủ (Imperial Household) with the Emperor deciding.
**1.2 Intake ≠ promotion.** 選秀 (draft of Eight-Banner girls) ran about every 3 years (Han girls were excluded). Daughters entered as Quý nhân/Thường tại/Đáp ứng (great houses higher, e.g. Phi/Tần for Mongol or top Manchu houses); it is the "3-year" cycle, and it is an INTAKE.
**1.3 Who decides, how.** The Emperor alone by 旨 (edict). The Thái hậu/Hoàng hậu advised and could ratify or block, especially for Phi and above and for the Hoàng hậu seat (Kangxi consulted Hiếu Trang; Qianlong's mother Xiaosheng). Nội vụ phủ drafted the decree (and for Phi/Tần and up the formal 冊文/冊寶 patent, with a 冊封 ceremony in the palace hall or in the consort's palace; Quý nhân and below normally got only a court/palace decree through Nội vụ phủ — to be double-checked per reign). No fixed review board, no exam.
**1.4 Occasions (ranked by how common).**
1. Birth of a child: the strongest road. A son (esp. first/eldest or an heir) often lifted the mother one grade within weeks to a year (Ming: Zheng Guifei, Shufei→Guifei on her son's birth ~1586; Qing: Wei Jiashi/Ling Fei climbed with her births). A daughter: smaller bump, often only after several births. A son who becomes heir/Emperor raises the mother massively (Thái phi/Thái hậu; Qianlong's Ula Nara and Xiaoyichun were raised by their son's accession).
2. Imperial favour (long-term 受寵): promotion of a favourite with no event, often 1 grade at a time, sometimes 2 grades in a year; a rare "leap" (e.g. Quý nhân→Tần in one decree) existed, but jumping ≥2 grades at once was exceptional and drew comment.
3. Grand occasions: 萬壽節 (Emperor's birthday), Thái hậu's round birthdays (50/60/70/80), 册立 of a new Empress, 登基 of a new Emperor (the new Emperor raised his mother, his own wives and the old guard "覃恩"; mourning periods blocked ceremony). These were the usual days for BATCH 晉封.
4. Merit/duty: a consort who ran the palace (攝六宮事), nursed the Emperor/Thái hậu, raised another's child, or was involved in an affair of state could be raised.
5. Deathbed / posthumous (晉封/追封): raised on the sickbed (Qianlong's Gao Jia consort made Hoàng quý phi in 1745 and died within days) or after death (追封, extremely common for mothers, favourites and for relatives of the Emperor).
6. Family: a father/brother's military or civil merit raised the consort (Yongzheng's Nian Shi rose with her brother Nian Gengyao's victories). The reverse is real too: the clan's disgrace brought demotion.
**1.5 Frequency.** Typical gap between steps was years (3–10), many consorts stayed at one grade for life; an Emperor might issue a batch of promotions a few times a decade. There was NO schedule.
**1.6 Demotion.** 降位/降封 (e.g. Empress Ula Nara cut her hair, 1765: title removed, never formally deposed in name, died 1766; Ming: Empress Hu deposed 1428 under Xuande), 貶為庶人 + 冷宮 for real crimes; demotion was an Imperial act too, often with a public edict.
**1.7 Ming differences.** Looser: no fixed seat caps; a 皇貴妃 tier; 册封 with a patent for Phi and above; promotion tied to Hoàng tử's birth even more strongly (the "母以子貴" rule, shared with Qing); 選秀 not on a fixed 3-year cycle but around the Emperor's marriage/selection campaigns.
**1.8 Drama tropes (NOT history).** 甄嬛传: whole careers decided by schemes, a promotion "for a dance/song/poem on a festival day", jumping 3 grades after a pregnancy. 延禧攻略: Wei Yingluo as a palace maid becoming Ling Fei by pure favour and cleverness (the real Wei Jiashi was a Nội vụ phủ bondmaid's daughter who entered as Quý nhân through the draft — sources differ on her first status). In history, "pregnancy → rank" is real; "maid → Phi" is rare; "whole court watches a contest to be promoted" is mostly drama.

**Answer to sếp's question.** Periodic 3-year review: NO. Promotion always by the Emperor's pleasure, clustered around occasions; "no occasion at all" was possible when the Emperor simply favoured her, but rank steps were usually timed with an event or with the Emperor's month of ceremonies.
