### A1. Real history — how a favour moved inside Ming/Qing harems
Sources: Minh sử · Hậu phi truyện; Thanh sử cảo · Hậu phi truyện; Quốc triều cung sử (国朝宫史, Càn Long, the Qing palace rule book: allowances and servant numbers by rank); Cung trung hiện hành tắc lệ (宫中现行则例); Evelyn Rawski, *The Last Emperors* (1998); Norman Kutcher, *Eunuch and Emperor in the Great Age of Qing Rule* (2018); Shih-shan Henry Tsai, *The Eunuchs in the Ming Dynasty* (1996); Lien-sheng Yang, "The Concept of Pao as a Basis for Social Relations in China" (1957); Hwang Kwang-kuo, "Face and Favor: The Chinese Power Game" (1987); Tây Kinh tạp ký (legend, Chiêu Quân).

| Fact | What it means for asking help |
|---|---|
| **Rank fixes everything you own**: Quốc triều cung sử gives each rank its allowance and its servants (Qing: Hoàng hậu 10 cung nữ, Hoàng quý phi / Quý phi 8, Phi / Tần 6, Quý nhân 4, Thường tại 3, Đáp ứng 2). | A low consort is POOR in hands and silver; she must borrow people and goods. The game's "price by rank" (BAL.nroad.bac by rank, staff caps) is historical. |
| **Chủ vị** (Qing): only Tần and above head a palace; Quý nhân / Thường tại / Đáp ứng live in its side halls under that mistress. | The first person a low consort asks is her own **chủ vị nương nương** — and the chủ vị can block, report or shield her. A lower woman asking OUTSIDE her chủ vị is itself a slight. |
| **Thỉnh an** each morning at the Hoàng hậu's palace; the Hoàng hậu (or a Hoàng quý phi "nhiếp lục cung sự") rules the harem. | Every harem favour passes the Trung cung's eyes; "nói giúp trước Hoàng hậu" is the normal channel, going around her is a risk. |
| **Kính sự phòng** eunuchs kept the bed record and the green-head tablets (lục đầu bài) the emperor turned after dinner. Bribing them to move a tablet is folk/drama lore (thin record), but the office was real. | The eunuch is the gate to the emperor's NIGHT; asking him costs silver ("môn bao", gate money, a real Qing custom for anyone who needed a door opened). |
| **Eunuchs barred from politics**: Thuận Trị's iron tablet (1655); Qing capped eunuch rank (Tổng quản ≈ tứ phẩm); carrying words or letters out of the palace was punished. Ming was the opposite: Ty Lễ giám and Đông Xưởng eunuchs ruled. | A letter OUT is dangerous for the messenger; a Qing-style eunuch refuses by **quy củ** ("nô tài không dám trái phép"); a Ming-style one helps for a price and holds the secret over you. |
| **Thái y viện kept mạch án** (pulse records; real Qing files survive). A hidden illness or a hidden pregnancy needs a doctor who will NOT write. | The doctor's refusal shape is "phải ghi sổ"; his price is his career; he is the classic **forced witness** later made to testify. |
| **Dưỡng mẫu**: Qing princes were often raised away from the birth mother (Ung Chính by Hiếu Ý Nhân hoàng hậu, not his mother). | Asking a high consort to raise / shelter your child is a real, huge favour — and a hostage. |
| **Ming, Thành Hóa: the hidden prince** (Minh sử). Maid Kỷ thị bears a son; Vạn quý phi orders it ended; a maid reports falsely, eunuch Trương Mẫn hides the baby, the deposed Ngô hoàng hậu helps raise him in secret for ~6 years; Trương Mẫn tells the emperor while combing his hair (1475). Kỷ thị soon dies suddenly; Trương Mẫn swallows gold. | One favour = a **chain of low helpers** (maid → eunuch → fallen empress), each with her own reason; the moment of telling is chosen (hair-combing, intimate hour); helpers pay with their lives AFTER the win. |
| **Ming, Nhâm Dần cung biến (1542)**: palace maids try to strangle Gia Tĩnh; Phương hoàng hậu saves him, then — while he cannot speak — has the innocent Đoan phi Tào thị executed with the plotters. | **Help used as a weapon**: the rescuer bills the rescue to destroy a rival. The emperor later blamed her (he did not save her from a fire, per Minh sử lore). |
| **Ban Tiệp dư (Hán)** refuses to ride in the emperor's palanquin, citing that wise kings rode with ministers, bad kings with favourites. | A favour from ABOVE can be refused only with a principle and great courtesy; the refusal itself earns Đức/Danh. |
| **Chiêu Quân & Mao Diên Thọ** (legend): the painter took bribes to paint maids fair; she would not pay, was painted plain and never summoned. | The **go-between's cut** is the hidden price; refusing to pay is a road of its own (with its own later reward). |
| **Bao (báo) / nhân tình** (Yang 1957; Hwang 1987): a favour creates a debt that must be returned, ideally larger ("thụ nhân điểm thủy chi ân, đương dũng tuyền tương báo"); refusing a person you owe is a double loss of face; asking costs face; a public refusal shames the asker. | j72's nợ ân ledger and "từ chối thì mếch lòng gấp đôi" are exactly bao. Writers should make the debt **talk** later ("ngày ấy tỷ đã giúp muội…"). |

**Who may refuse whom (history, condensed):**
- Higher → lower: may refuse openly; may also "grant" a favour that is really an order.
- Equal ranks: refuse with an excuse (sickness, the chủ vị, the rules); open refusal = open enmity.
- Lower → higher: may NOT refuse openly (bất kính). Shapes: delay (cáo bệnh), plead no ability, pass it upward ("xin bẩm chủ vị nương nương"), or agree and do it badly.
- Servant → own master: no refusal; only silence, delay, or secret betrayal.
- Servant → another consort: refuses by naming her master ("nô tỳ phải bẩm chủ tử trước").
- Officials / Thái y / eunuchs → any consort: refuse by **quy củ** (law, records, the emperor's order) — the one refusal no rank can punish openly.

### A2. Palace dramas — how they stage asking for help, half-help and betrayal
Sources: Hậu cung Chân Hoàn truyện (2011); Diên Hi công lược (2018); Hậu cung Như Ý truyện (2018); Đại Minh cung từ (2000); Đại Trường Kim (2003).

| Chân Hoàn | Chân Hoàn helps An Lăng Dung rise (patronage, introductions); An Lăng Dung, jealous and afraid, later hands her a scar salve laced with musk. | **Kẻ được giúp cắn lại** — the helped one's envy grows with the debt. |

| Đại Minh cung từ | Thái Bình asks her mother Võ hậu for the man she wants; the mother grants it by having his wife removed. Uyển Nhi stands between mother and daughter, ally and rival by turns. | **Ân trên trút họa xuống người thứ ba** — the favour is paid by someone else. |
| Đại Trường Kim | Officials and kitchen ladies sell the Thượng cung seat for a clan; Hàn thượng cung refuses to cheat and is destroyed, later avenged. | **Đút lót ngôi vị**; **từ chối vì đạo, báo đáp đời sau**. |

**Drama staging rules** (what makes the scene work on screen):
1. The ask is **indirect**: tea, a gift, a story about "a friend", a kneel only at the last. The real request is said once, quietly.
2. The helper's **reply line is the tell** — "Muội cứ yên tâm" said too fast is the drama's sign of half-help.
3. The **price is named after** the help, never before ("ngày sau tỷ có việc cần đến muội…").
4. Betrayal is set up **two or three scenes earlier** with a small, true sign (a grievance, a gift refused, a glance at a rival) — never from nowhere.
5. The reveal comes **late, at a bad time**, through a third person (a maid's confession, a found letter, a doctor's record).

### A3. Games — how a companion's side line becomes a process
Sources: Mass Effect 2 (BioWare 2010); Persona 3/4/5 (Atlus 2006–2016); The Witcher 3 (CD Projekt Red 2015); Dragon Age: Origins (BioWare 2009); Crusader Kings III (Paradox 2020).

| Game | Mechanism | Lesson for Q1/Q2/Q3 |
|---|---|---|
| **Mass Effect 2 — loyalty missions** | Each companion brings ONE personal problem rooted in their own past; the mission is about THAT story; doing it makes them loyal (a power + survival at the finale). Two pairs clash (Jack/Miranda, Tali/Legion): siding with one costs the other unless your standing is high. | Q1: the ask must be **about the NPC's chapter conflict**, not a stock errand. Q2: the reward lands at the **NPC's finale**. Taking a side in a clash between two NPCs loses one of them. |
| **Persona confidants / social links** | 10 ranks; each rank = a short scene in that person's own story; your answers shape the bond; each rank unlocks a perk; P3/P4 links can **reverse** or break if you betray or neglect them. | Q2: a visible **ledger of lines she has joined** (name, chapter reached, result) = the Persona list; a betrayal visibly turns the line. |
| **The Witcher 3 — secondary quests** | Chains with **delayed consequences** (Bloody Baron: the result of your choice shows weeks later); the quest-giver may lie; payment can be **haggled**; a "monster" can be the victim. | Q2 "hồi âm": the result of her help reaches her later, in the NPC's next chapter. Q1: the asker may hide part of the truth (true info only — HANDOVER L46 — so it must be a hidden truth fixed at creation, revealed honestly). |
| **Dragon Age: Origins — approval** | Gifts and choices move each companion's approval; low approval → they leave, or (Zevran) betray at a later meeting. | Q3: the helper's **liking** is the strongest term; betrayal waits for a later trigger. |
| **Crusader Kings III — hooks & schemes** | Granting a favour gives a **weak hook** ("owes a favour"); a hook can force someone to agree. Schemes use agents; each agent adds success AND discovery risk; a forced agent leaks more. | Exactly OD:L1601 (5): a **forced helper** (one she holds nợ ân or a secret over) agrees but does it worse and may tell. nợ ân = CK3 hook. |

**Game rules worth copying:** one personal problem per companion; the help is a scene in THEIR story; reward and loyalty show later; refusals and betrayals change the companion visibly; a forced helper raises both the odds and the risk of being found out.
