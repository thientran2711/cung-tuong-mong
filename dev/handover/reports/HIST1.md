# HIST1 — Qing prince (a ca 阿哥) rooms: motif, colour, residence
depth: worker of ROOT-C4 → results sent to ROOT-C5 + PLACES3 · START_CTX ~8 % · owner L2384 (no dragons, no royal yellow for a ca)

## 1. Motif
- 龍 (5-claw "long") = emperor only (Sotheby's "Dragon Robe Decoded"; Mangfu/Wikipedia). Princes' robes are officially *mang* 蟒 "python robes", never "dragon robes" (Sotheby's lot notes).
- Qing rule (Qinding huangchao liqi tushi 1759): emperor's sons & 1st/2nd-rank princes could get a 5-claw *mang* (looks like a dragon, different name); grandsons / 3rd rank and below = 4-claw mang. So historically a ca did NOT carve "dragons".
- To a player's eye a 5-claw mang = a dragon. Owner's rule (L2384: "rồng chỉ có vua") → for game rooms use NO dragon-shaped beast at all, not even mang. Use instead:
  麒麟 qilin (a prince/learned son, "麒麟送子"), 蝙蝠 bats (福), 祥雲 clouds, 回紋 meander key-fret, 卷草 scrolls, 蓮/牡丹/梅蘭竹菊 flowers, 葫蘆/石榴 (many sons), 松鶴, 博古 antiques, books/scrolls, carp/fish (科舉). Lions 獅 allowed (paired, small).
- FORBIDDEN in a ca rooms: 龍 any claw count, 龍鳳 pair, 鳳 phoenix (empress/consort), 十二章 symbols, nine-dragon screens, pearl-chasing dragons on bed/canopy.

## 2. Colour
- 明黃 bright yellow = emperor (+ empress/dowager). 杏黃 apricot = crown prince. 金黃 golden yellow = emperor's sons (皇子) on robes; other princes blue/石青 or brown unless granted (Kyoto Nat. Museum glossary; Sotheby's; HKU UMAG catalogue notes blue for 1st/2nd-rank princes).
- So strictly, a 皇子 robe could be 金黃 — but this is clothing, and owner wants no yellow in a ca rooms. Game rule: NO yellow silk/curtains/bedding; gold allowed only as thin restrained trim (gilt edges, brass fittings), never gilded panels.
- Prince palette: 石青 deep blue-black, 藍 blue, 綠 green (East = wood/growth), 醬紫/赭 brown, cinnabar-red lacquer pillars, natural 紫檀/花梨 wood, white paper windows.

## 3. Residence (阿哥所 / 南三所 / 乾西五所 / 擷芳殿)
- 南三所 rebuilt Qianlong 11 (1746) on the 擷芳殿 site, east of the Outer Court, princes' residences (offpeaktrip note; widely cited). Commonly cited green glazed roofs (rank below yellow; China Daily: princes' mansions = green tiles) — one travel note says yellow; treat green as the common version.
- Three parallel courtyards, each front hall + rear bedroom, modest size: a school-age boy's home, more scholar's study than palace. Luxury = good hardwood, carved lattice, books, a 炕 bed with plain silk, few porcelains. NOT a throne room, no gold walls.
- Princes studied at 上書房 (Shangshufang): plain, scholarly, desks, bookcases, couplets → "moderate", matches owner "too much".

## 4. Dramas
- Diên Hi Công Lược (延禧攻略 2018): praised for Palace-Museum colour palette; prince/consort spaces muted, low-saturation, mostly wood + blue/green; dragons mainly around the emperor.
- Như Ý Truyện (如懿传 2018): princes in 石青/blue robes, small mang badges; a-ca rooms simple wood, books. Both keep yellow for emperor/empress-dowager.

## PROMPT RULE (prince / a ca rooms — bedroom, reception, Shangshufang)
```
PRINCE ROOM RULE: Qing prince (a-ge) quarters, NOT imperial. Absolutely no dragons of any kind (no long, no mang, no dragon-and-phoenix, no phoenix).
Carvings/embroidery use only: qilin, bats among auspicious clouds, key-fret (huiwen) borders, scrolling grass, lotus/peony/plum-orchid-bamboo-chrysanthemum, gourds and pomegranates, pine and crane, books and antiques.
No yellow silk, curtains or bedding; palette = stone-blue (shiqing), muted green, deep indigo, warm brown hardwood (zitan/huali), cinnabar-red lacquer, white paper lattice windows.
Gold only as thin restrained trim and brass fittings, never gilded panels or gold walls.
Moderate scholarly luxury: elegant but restrained, uncluttered, fewer ornaments, books and calligraphy; quieter than the emperor's or consorts' rooms.
```
