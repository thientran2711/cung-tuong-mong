## 1. REAL COURTS
### 1.1 Qing (清) [DOC for the system, MIX for the proportions]
- Palace maids 宫女 were Manchu-banner (Bao-yi, 包衣) girls levied in the Imperial Household Department, normally released at ~25. A maid favoured by the Emperor could be given a consort rank; the lowest ranks were 答应 (Dá-yìng), 常在 (Cháng-zài), 贵人 [DOC]. 官女子 is the formal term for the lowest unranked-upward "maid-class lady" below those ranks; it appears in Qing records for girls who had been favoured but not yet formally created [MIX: the label is documented, how long she stayed in it varies by reign].
- The Jingshifang (敬事房) kept the intimacy register: date, who, "留 / 不留" and pregnancy reports, so a night was evidence a mother could later use [DOC that the office and register existed]. Rank was usually given after pregnancy or at the Emperor's / Empress Dowager's pleasure; many maid-favourites were created 常在/答应 within weeks or months, not instantly [MIX].
- Famous maid-rising: the model of Wei Yingluo in dramas is Empress Xiaoyichun (魏佳氏, Jiaqing's mother), a Bao-yi maid-class girl who rose from the lowest tier to Noble Consort and was posthumously Empress [DOC outline]. Also Kangxi's mother-line story of 辛者库 (Xinzhe-ku laundry bureau) origin: Consort Liang (良妃), mother of the 8th prince Yinsi, was from that bureau [DOC]. Lesson: low origin hurt the CHILD's prospects at court, even when the child was a prince.
- Children of maid-mothers were still Emperor's sons (阿哥) and princesses, with no formal "illegitimate" label; the penalty was social: low mother rank -> low allowance, poor household, and often being RAISED by a higher consort (养育, e.g. given to an Empress or Noble Consort) [MIX]. Mother and child were frequently separated.
- Danger for unranked mothers: a maid who conceived without rank depended on the register to be believed (see Wanli). Rivals / Empress could get rid of her quietly [POP, plausible, dramas exaggerate].

### 1.2 Ming (明) [DOC, strongest evidence]
- Emperor Wanli (万历) slept with a maid (later Consort Wang 王恭妃) of his mother's palace; she became pregnant; Wanli DENIED it. Empress Dowager Li produced the palace diary 《内起居注》 as proof and said, in effect, that even Wanli himself was born of a maid (his own mother Li was a maid of Longqing) and "mother is honoured by son" 母以子贵. He gave in, she was made 恭妃 [DOC: Ming Shi, the diary is documented]. She bore the future Taichang Emperor. Lessons for the game: (a) a maid-night is recorded, (b) pregnancy forces the question of rank, (c) the Emperor may refuse and a senior woman (Thái hậu) can force it, (d) the child can later become heir.
- Hongzhi Emperor's mother Lady Ji (纪氏), a maid of non-Han origin, bore the child in secret to hide from Consort Wan; she was later honoured but died young and mysteriously [DOC outline, details MIX]. Shows the "hidden pregnancy of an unranked maid" danger.
- Ming 彤史 (Tongshi) also recorded menstruation and pregnancy, which helped decide whether a night "counted" [DOC].

### 1.3 Joseon (朝鮮) [DOC for the institution]
- 궁녀 (court ladies) belonged to a rank-and-file corps. A court lady who spent a night with the King became 승은상궁 (承恩尚宮, "grace-receiving sanggung") -> 특별상궁 (special sanggung), a HALF-rank: she was no longer an ordinary maid but not yet a royal concubine (후궁 with a title like 숙원). She often stayed in this state for years [DOC].
- Becoming a royal concubine (숙원, 소훈, ...) usually required bearing a child, or special favour. Dong Yi (淑嬪崔氏) was a court maid of lowest birth who rose this way and bore Yeongjo [DOC outline]. Gwanghaegun's mother Gongbin Kim was a court lady -> concubine; his own succession shows a maid-born child could even take the throne [DOC].
- Children of concubines were royal sons (군) / princesses (옹주) but ranked below the Queen's sons; birth through a low concubine hurt heir chances [DOC]. This is the closest historical match to the owner's rule: night -> unranked "half-rank" -> rank when conditions allow.

### 1.4 Vietnam, Nguyễn (阮) [MIX; weakest]
- Cung nữ / thị nữ served in the Nội cung; the Emperor could take one and give her the lowest consort title (Cung nhân, then Tài nhân / Mỹ nhân up the ladder). Gia Long to Tự Đức had large harems; Minh Mạng had many consorts of modest origin. Specific cases of one night followed by delayed rank are not well fixed in my memory: treat as flavour only [MIX/POP].

### 1.5 Sources of "who is called": the three patterns in real courts
1. The Emperor sees a maid himself (tea, bedchamber duty) [common, DOC in outline].
2. A consort or the Empress offers a maid to keep her own position when she cannot serve (pregnant, ill, in mourning, confined) — the "通房"/"陪嫁" practice at household level and the "tiến cử" at court [MIX].
3. A Thái hậu or an elder pushes a maid to the Emperor (Wanli's maid was of his mother's palace) [DOC].

## 2. HOW DRAMAS SHOW IT [POP]
- Yanxi Palace (延禧攻略): Wei Yingluo, a maid, is noticed by the Emperor after tea / a small act and rises 答应 -> 贵人 -> ... — rapid, glamorous, and enemy-filled. Matches "raised at once".
- Ruyi's Royal Love (如懿传): the same actress's rival climb shows a maid who ranks via the Emperor's whim and a patron; the unranked state is not shown.
- Empresses in the Palace (后宫·甄嬛传): schemers use maids as bait, and a maid-night is a political move by the owner of that maid; rank follows quickly because the show favours drama over registry.
- Dong Yi / Jewel in the Palace (Joseon): the two dramas show 승은 as a lonely half-state: the court lady who has "received grace" is watched, jealous and exposed; her pregnancy is the thing that makes her ranked and also gets her attacked. This matches the owner's "no rank until a seat" picture the best.
- Common beats: (1) the maid is noticed by accident; (2) a senior consort tries to stop or poison her; (3) she is promoted for pregnancy; (4) her child is taken or threatened; (5) her old mistress is either her ally or her worst enemy. All five map to systems the game already has (loyalty, ghen, hại).

## 3. HOW OTHER GAMES HANDLE IT (general knowledge, unverified)
- Palace sim / gacha games (e.g. Empress in the Palace style mobile games, various "harem" sims): a "maid served the Emperor" event usually gives an immediate low rank and no unranked state; a child-from-low-mother penalty is rare (often child is just "lower affinity"). Most games reduce the whole thing to a flat chance at promotion.
- Crusader Kings style: a low-status mother gives the child a status penalty (bastard / concubine child) and a legitimacy stat. The idea of "child's lower status changes inheritance weight but not nothing" is standard and cheap.
- Takeaway: the owner's version (unranked mother, child with lower status, promotion later when a seat frees) is MORE nuanced than most games and matches Joseon best. It is a genuinely good fit for the court dramas she likes.

## 4. PROPOSED GAME RULE (plain words, suggested numbers; all subject to the owner and the 14:20Z time review)
Name: **Thị tẩm cung nữ (maid night)**. One rule that REPLACES sendMaid 3 %, the srvRise weekly roll and the NPC staff rise.

A. WHEN. On each flip-day (today 3 per month, ~1 per 10 days), after the 1st/15th Empress check and BEFORE the 90 % tray roll: the Emperor calls a maid instead of turning a card with chance **10 %** (about 0.3 per month, about 3-4 per year; about 1 flip-day in 10). Modifiers: +5 % when he is bored of the tray (best card weight below a threshold), +5 % when a Thái hậu / Hoàng hậu pushes ("tiến cử"), −5 % when the Empress is in favour that month; hard cap 18 %. If the owner raises the number of flip-days per month (14:20Z), scale so that the monthly average stays about 0.3. Keeps Empress 1st/15th untouched and keeps the card tray as the main road.
B. WHO. Weights: his own palace maids 40 %; a maid of a consort 45 %, weighted by that consort's Sủng and the maid's Sắc/loyalty (so a favourite's maid is more likely); a maid of the Empress/Thái hậu 15 %. She must be aged 18+. THE PLAYER: (1) if she is on the servant road, she is one of the possible maids with the same roll, weighted by her nt/Sắc and her mistress's malice (this replaces the weekly 1.2 % + nt/25 roll); (2) if she is a consort, her own maids are candidates AND she has the existing action "Tiến cử cung nữ hầu thay" when she is pregnant / ill / out of favour — an offer, not an obligation; the Emperor may also notice one of her maids on his own (she may lose the maid's loyalty or gain an ally).
C. WHAT HAPPENS NEXT, by odds (seat free means `roomFor("consort")` true):
   - Seat free: **50 %** raised at once to the lowest rank (rank 0), **50 %** left unranked ("thị tẩm", Joseon 승은상궁 style).
   - Seats full: **100 %** unranked (no silver-and-out; she stays in the palace as a "cung nữ có ơn").
D. UNRANKED STATE ("chưa phong"). She keeps her post as maid (no seat used), cannot be a card on the tray, gets a small hidden favour bonus (+Sủng, no Sủng card), and sits in a waiting list (cap 3 so it cannot clog). Per month while a seat is free: **25 %** she is considered; if she has borne a child: **60 %**. Candidate she is raised to rank 0 (not above, unless the Thái hậu / owner pushes). If she waits more than 12 months with no child and no seat she is sent out with silver (the old ending).
E. PREGNANCY while unranked. Use a lower base than a consort: **12 %** per night (consorts keep `conceiveCh`), or the same formula × 0.6. Pregnant unranked mother: hidden by default (like `hidden`), small risk of loss from jealousy (5-10 %, mitigated by a protector or the Emperor). When revealed: she is raised to rank 0 immediately if a seat is free; otherwise she is kept as "dưỡng thai" and promoted at the first freed seat (priority 1).
F. THE CHILD ("thứ xuất"). Still a prince / princess. Differences: (1) heir weight × 0.5 (never zero — the Wanli / Gwanghaegun precedents); (2) 35 % the child is given to a higher consort to be raised if the mother is unranked at birth (history: 抱养); (3) allowance smaller; (4) if the mother is raised later, the child's mark disappears after the mother reaches rank 1+ (母以子贵). Cheap to implement as a flag `lowborn` on the child.
G. SYMMETRY / CO-OP. Whatever the player can do (offer her maid, ride a maid night) NPC consorts do too (this is the same sendMaid roll for NPC mistresses; weights by personality and mood, OD 14:10Z). The roll is host-only in co-op; both players see the same announcement; each player's maids and servant-road character are equal candidates; no player gets a guaranteed or exclusive slot.
H. NPC reactions. The consort whose card was skipped loses nothing (the Emperor did not choose another card); a consort whose maid was called gets ghen from others, but also an ally/enemy shift on the maid (loyal >= 60 -> grateful, else turns away — the existing logic).

## 5. BLIND SPOTS / RISKS (likelihood x severity)
1. **Unranked state does not exist in code** [likely 90 %, severity LARGE]. Rivals/consorts all carry `rank` and take seats; staff maids have no kids, no pregnancy, no `birthP`. Easiest safe path: keep unranked maids as STAFF with a flag (`ân` = chưa phong) and extend the birth/child code with `momUnranked`; DO NOT make them rank −1 rivals (that breaks `roomFor`, `liveRivals`, palFix, tray, save). Detect by test: full-game simulation with 20 maid nights; fix by gating birth code on the flag.
2. **Heir / pregnancy balance (D57) inflation** [possible 40 %, severity MEDIUM]. Maid pregnancies add children outside the seat cap; heirs outnumber what the pregnancy model expects. Mitigate: low conceive (12 %), 0.5 heir weight, cap 3 unranked, review with the night-time analyst's numbers; detect with the monthly heir-count in a sim.
3. **Servant-road consent and safety** [possible 30 %, severity LARGE]. The player is a maid summoned for a night; the Phuong Lenh safety rules (adult only, no coercion shown) are the model; she must be 18+, event text must give her a CHOICE (accept / ask her mistress / excuse) with consequences, not an auto-event, and no depiction beyond the fade. Detect: a safety review of the event text; fix: make "decline" always possible with an outcome (Tin -x, but no punishment beyond that).
4. **Seat starvation and queue clog** [possible 35 %, severity MEDIUM]. With CAPC = 16 and death/exile as the only freeing event, unranked maids pile up and become a frustrating dead end. Cap 3, 12-month sunset, priority to mothers; consider freeing seats via the existing exam/demotion flow. Detect: count of unranked maids in long sims.
5. **Maid pool drain and loyalty chaos** [possible 30 %, severity SMALL]. Each call removes a maid from the pool (roomFor("maid") is based on a pool); a favourite's maid being called can flip her loyalty. Refill via `refillPool`; the loyalty flip is a feature (drama). Hard to break; easy to tune.
6. **Co-op divergence** [rare 10 %, severity MEDIUM]. Host-only roll + both clients show a different "night" screen; if the tray flip at evening start (14:00Z #4) and maid-night both resolve on the host, the order must be fixed in one place (maid check BEFORE the card pick) or the tray shows a card and then cancels. Detect: 2-client test.
