# HIST2 — Qing palace windows / doors / curtains (owner L2387: "cửa sổ là không có rèm")
depth 5 (worker of ROOT-C5) · START_CTX ~3 % · no images, no gens · finished, no handover needed

## Verdict
Owner is right. **Fabric drapes hanging at windows (side-gathered curtains, swags, valances, tie-backs) = European, NOT Qing.** Qing windows are self-covering: the lattice itself is the "curtain". Fabric in Qing rooms hangs at DOORS, around BEDS/炕, and at internal PARTITION OPENINGS — never as drapes framing a window.

## What was at each opening (Forbidden City, consort palaces, 阿哥所, studies, servants' rooms)
1. **Windows** (槛窗 sill windows, 支摘窗 prop-up/remove windows, 隔扇 lattice door-panels): carved wooden lattice (步步锦, 灯笼框, 冰裂纹, 万字, 回纹) covered with **窗纸 / 高丽纸** (tough Korean mulberry paper) in winter, **纱 / 冷布** (gauze, often green or blue) in summer — the palace custom "冬糊纸，夏糊纱", renewed by the 内务府 every season. Light comes in soft and diffuse; no view, so no curtain needed.
2. **Glass** (玻璃): small glass panes set in the centre of the lattice from Yongzheng/Qianlong (养心殿 etc.), widespread in the inner palaces by late Qing (Tongzhi–Guangxu: 储秀宫, 长春宫, 乐寿堂). Still lattice-framed; still no hanging drapes (at most a small inner paper/silk roll shade, rare — skip in game).
3. **Doors** (main 隔扇 door with a **帘架** curtain frame in front): **门帘** — winter **棉门帘** (padded, cloth/brocade, often blue or red with a wooden bar), summer **竹帘 / 湘帘** (fine bamboo slat blinds, sometimes painted, with silk edging). These are the ONLY "curtains" a viewer sees at the front of a hall.
4. **Eaves / veranda** (廊): **竹帘 / 苇帘** roll-up bamboo or reed blinds and summer **凉棚** awnings over courtyards; rolled up by day.
5. **Beds**: 架子床 / 拔步床 / 炕 with **床帐 / 幔帐** (silk or gauze bed curtains, hooked back with 帐钩), 炕罩 canopy-frame around the kang. Correct — KEEP.
6. **Partition openings** inside rooms: 落地罩, 花罩, 栏杆罩, 圆光罩, 碧纱橱 (gauze-pasted partition doors). Openings may carry a **软帘 / 帷幔** (soft silk hanging, usually tied back) — inside the room, between spaces, never on the exterior window wall.
7. **Servants' rooms / 值房**: same lattice + paper, plainer lattice, cotton 棉门帘, no silk.

## How to keep rooms rich without drapes (owner: no room may look poor)
Richness goes into: densely carved lattice with gilded or lacquered frames, glowing paper/gauze light, carved 花罩 with silk 帷幔 tied back, embroidered bed 帐, brocade 门帘 / painted 湘帘 at the door, 炕 cushions and 靠背 in silk, 多宝格, screens, porcelain, hanging lanterns, incense burners.

## PROMPT RULE
```
WINDOW RULE (Qing palace): windows are carved wooden lattice (bubu-jin, ice-crack, key-fret) backed by glowing white paper or pale gauze, optionally small glass panes in the lattice centre; NO fabric curtains, drapes, swags, valances or tie-backs at any window.
Doors: lattice doors with a curtain frame; allowed = padded brocade door curtain (winter) or fine painted bamboo blind (summer); roll-up bamboo/reed blinds only under eaves.
Beds and kang: silk or gauze bed curtains hooked back with metal hooks = allowed and encouraged.
Inside partitions (carved openwork luodizhao/huazhao arches): soft silk hangings tied back are allowed, never on the exterior window wall.
Keep the room rich: densely carved gilded or lacquered lattice, warm diffused light through paper, embroidered bed curtains, silk cushions, screens, curio shelves, porcelain, lanterns.
Negative: european curtains, window drapes, curtain rods, pelmets, ruffles, net curtains.
```
